## Changelog

| **0.4.2** | Training-quality pass on the embedder scale: the recipe that worked on the Slowosiec chart (norm ~0.8, chart rms 0.024) was ported to a retriever-scale chart (norm ~24, chart rms 0.99) with constants that are only calibrated for the former, and it silently stopped working: the same-embedder control run (S1) measured `eval/mrr_lift` −0.013 at epoch 12, a flat closed-loop holonomy (0.002 against 0.120 on the reference) and `C_rs` stuck at its 0.5 prior. Three measured defects, three fixes: (1) **the l2 losses are not scale-free** — curvature, shadow and cycle are absolute distances inside the operator chart, so the port took the total loss from 0.33 to 13.5 and the anti-commutation hinge from dead to dominant; every quadratic term now divides by the square and every linear one by itself of a *measured* chart unit (`chart_rms / REFERENCE_CHART_RMS`, printed at startup and persisted in `config.json`; 1.0 on the reference reproduces the historical loss exactly, `--no-l2-unit-auto` / `--l2-scale` to opt out or pin it); (2) **`REFERENCE_COS_STD` was a guessed 0.047 against the reference run's measured 0.1477 raw / 0.1400 centred**, so `--geometry-auto` inflated `contrast_temperature`, `anchor_margin` and `knn_slack` ~3x on every other space (measured: T 0.1579 against 0.053 at the reference ratio, the contrast term at 75–80 % of the loss near chance while the refinement destroyed the index); the reference is now the measured F4 profile, selected raw or centred the same way the chart is; (3) **`C_rs` never moved inside a run** — `num_rel²` bounded logits taught by a 16-pair witness sample at the base lr (measured: every cell 0.500 ± 0.000 after 12 epochs); it now has its own param group with `comm_lr_scale` (default 20), the same recipe as the rewriting dial. A per-epoch loss-term breakdown was added to the train log — a dead or dominant term is the common failure mode here, and it is invisible in a total. Screening (same-embedder data, otherwise identical recipe, same seed): epoch 12 `eval/mrr` 0.0275 → 0.0490 and `mrr_lift` −0.0132 → −0.0006; a 30-epoch run that reaches F4 (S4) lands at `eval/mrr` **0.157** (0.038 unfixed), `mrr_lift` **+0.065** (−0.007), `rel_acc_macro` 0.447 (0.382), `prod_mrr` 0.338 (0.289), with `C_rs` now spread over [0.30, 0.94] and all six fixture order pairs separated from the 0.000 translational floor (median 0.306). New regression tests pin the l2 scale invariance (including the fixed-margin vs `margin_auto` distinction), the measured cosine reference (raw vs centred selection) and the chart-unit measurement. Known remaining defect, independent of scale: the closed-loop holonomy stays ~0 (0.0003 on S4) — the curvature term contributes no gradient, so the connection has no measurable curvature yet; see "co dalej" below. **Co dalej (next):** the curvature signal needs a real teacher — either witness-weighted `L_commut` cells (up-weighting graph antonymia edges, the only non-commutativity the graph actually shows) or a holonomy regulariser that rewards non-flat loops for pairs `C_rs` marks as non-commuting; the contrast term remains the largest loss at 58–79 % of the total and a hard-negative variant (top-k of the model's own ranking instead of all 182 960 keys) is the obvious next lever for `mrr_lift` on high-duplicate spaces (53.7 % of the nodes here are 1-NN duplicates). |
| **0.4.1** | Consistency diagnostics (ideas/12) as a runnable, embedder-independent module. New package `relgat_projector/core/diagnostics/`: the transport is the only model-facing interface, so the same bilingual fixtures run on any embedder and language with either the learned operator (`CheckpointTransport`, charted once through `prepare`) or the embedder-free translation baseline (`AdditiveTransport`, commutative by construction and therefore scoring order separation and holonomy of exactly 0 -- the floor every learned operator has to beat). 12a: per-document `S` (self-negation), `K` (inner coherence), closed-loop holonomy (the loop is undone with the unmodulated inverse, the same convention the curvature loss uses), the surface-negation baseline, and raw claim cosine, plus a gold-pair AUC over the fixture's by-construction contradiction pairs. 12b: order separation `1 - cos(T_s T_r x, T_r T_s x)` over relation pairs against the learned `C_rs`, with the 0.5 "no evidence" band counted explicitly. New app `relgat_projector_apps.diagnostics.consistency_report` (fixture + `--best` auto-resolves the best checkpoint by scanning `report.json`), `make_consistency_fixture` and the bilingual fixtures themselves: 13 documents (54 claims, 13 gold contradictions) in Polish and English, 84 order tasks, and a 69+69 seed-pair lexicon of the six lexical relations. First measurement on F4 (selection score 0.3361) and `ipipan/silver-retriever-base-v1.1`: the learned operator is strongly non-commutative (median order separation 0.188 vs the 0.000 floor; closed-loop defect 0.120) and its `C_rs` points the right way on the relations that matter (spearman(sep, C) = -0.45 on the six fixture relations), while the surface-negation baseline (AUC 0.857 PL) still beats the transport-based `S` (0.762) -- and the same documents scored in English give AUC 0.357, a measured, quantified asymmetry of a Polish-centric space. The `training_scripts/run-wiazka-trainer.sh` recipe is now the pinned, reproducible recipe of the best run (F4): `--op-dim 256` (pinned, since auto would resolve to 128 here), `--knn-weight 1.0 --knn-k 8 --knn-slack 0.02`, `--calib-ratio 0.5`, `--qr-max-queries 256`, with provenance and the re-scoring / health-check commands in its header. |
| **0.4.0** | Embedder independence + honest checkpoint selection. The method now *measures* the base space it is given instead of assuming one: `EmbedderProfile` (`relgat_projector/core/geometry/embedder.py`) records unit-norm, random-pair cosine mean/std/p99 (as stored and mean-centred), anisotropy, the singular-value explained-variance curve (32/64/128/256/512), effective rank, and the exact-duplicate 1-NN mass; it is computed at trainer startup, printed, and stored in `config.json`/`report.json`. `--op-dim 0` (the new default) derives the operator dimension from that spectrum (smallest dimension keeping `--op-dim-variance` of the variance, bounded by `--op-dim-cap`), which also removes the historical `ValueError` that made the method unable to run at all on any embedder with at most 256 dimensions. `--base-center` builds the operator chart on the mean-centred base (the chart origin moves to the data centroid; `base_to_op` becomes affine, the export adds the centre back, so the retriever index stays exactly the stored vectors); measured on the Słowosieć the anisotropy drops 0.1323 -> 0.0006. `--geometry-auto` rescales the cosine knobs (`contrast_temperature`, `anchor_margin`, `knn_slack`) by the ratio of this space's random-pair cosine std to the reference scale they were tuned on (`REFERENCE_COS_STD`), reproducing the shipped defaults exactly on that space. Honest calibration: `--calib-ratio 0.5` splits the held-out set into a slice the served scalars (dial, relation prior, ensemble weight) are fitted on and a remainder the metrics are scored on; fitting them on the supervised edges was fitting them on the edges the decoder memorised, and a relation with fewer than 24 calibration edges falls back to supervised edges for its dial. Best-checkpoint selection was measured on the wrong quantity: it contained `prod_mrr` computed with the gradient-trained dial, which never leaves `lam ~ 0.8`, so on F2 the criterion ranked weights whose traffic metric had already decayed (0.4696 at epoch 15 -> 0.4467 at epoch 60) as the best of the run. `evaluate()` now reports `eval/prod_mrr_traffic_bestlam` (best *global* rewriting strength on the traffic probe — dial-invariant, a lower bound on the calibrated model, `0.0` = the raw baseline) and the selection score is 0.30·mrr + 0.18·mrr_macro + 0.10·prod_mrr + 0.24·prod_mrr_traffic_bestlam + 0.18·rel_acc_macro_ensemble. `score_checkpoints.py` gains `--calib-ratio`, the `prod_tr_bl`/`lam_tr`/`score` columns, and a `source` field in its calibration entry. F4 (F2 config + `--calib-ratio 0.5` + the new selection score) is the first run both trained and selected by the honest criterion and is the first whose shipped calibration is already optimal (shipped = refit to the last digit, where F2 still left prod 0.2306 → 0.2342 on the table). Measured dead ends, documented not implemented: a per-query learned λ gate (held-out −0.0056; the gate collapses to a constant), a learned per-relation translation on top of the exported transport (held-out −0.010; the rotation family, not the missing shift, is the bottleneck), and feeding the refined node into the production path (0.2165 against 0.2307 served — the message passing does not help the query). |

| **0.3.1** | WIĄZKA training-quality pass, second round. The connection was numerically dead in every prior run: `log_eps` was initialised at −6, so ε ≈ 0.0025 ≈ 1 % of the rotation angle, and a full run moved it to −5.43 — base-point-dependent transport `θ(r, x) = θ_r + ε·P(relu(Wx))` effectively never trained. `log_eps` now starts at −2 (ε ≈ 0.135) and is logged as `eval/eps` every epoch. `C_rs` (commutation matrix) is learned from an **exhaustive** commutation-witness table over the real graph (the old random sampler found 3 % of the diamonds it needed): the unsupervised holonomy-gap gradient is detached from `C_rs` (supervision only) and is taught by class-balanced `supervision()` on witnessed pairs; curvature parameters moved to a no-weight-decay group. The λ dial is actually usable now: it lives in a dedicated optimizer group with `dial_lr_scale = 20`, and `calibrate_transport_dial()` fits the per-relation strength on **train** edges through the exact production protocol before final evaluation (5 s); `report.json` keeps `final_metrics` (calibrated) vs `final_metrics_trained_dial` so overfitting is visible. Relation prediction: fast batched `transport_relation_scores` (verified identical to the per-relation loop), `relation_logits(..., mode=decoder|prior|transport|ensemble)`, class-balanced `DecoderLoss` (`--decoder-balance`, default 0.5, matching the sampling temper) with `--decoder-label-smoothing` (default 0.05), active from F2 instead of only F4, batched `relation_matrices()` in the operator; `RelationFlow.classify_relation` / `transport_relation_scores` public. Curriculum: the F3 gate was `pce_ratio_k2 ≤ 1.6`, mathematically unreachable (two independent errors give ratio ≈ 2.0; best measured 2.07) — every run exited F3 through the `max_steps` valve and lost a third of the run before `L_pull` ever ran. New gate: `eval/path_lift ≥ 0` (two-hop transport MRR vs identity on a fixed pool; `eval/path_mrr`, `eval/path_mrr_ident`, `eval/path_lift` added). Best-checkpoint score: 0.42·mrr + 0.25·mrr_macro + 0.18·prod_mrr + 0.15·rel_acc_macro_ensemble. `report.json` now also carries the full per-epoch `history` (loss terms + eval metrics) and a per-relation table with `cls` (ensemble relation accuracy) and `lam` (dial). The frequency correction of the relation head was equally hardcoded: `mode="prior"` always subtracted `log p_train(r)`, which is the correct Bayes correction only for a head trained with plain cross-entropy -- applied to the class-balanced head it inverts the intended adjustment and scored macro **0.058** against 0.465 uncorrected (the balanced head fits `p(r|x)·w(r)`, so the correction it needs is `+beta·log count`, the opposite sign). It is now `log_softmax(logits) - strength·log p_train(r)` with `relation_prior_strength` fitted on train edges (`fit_relation_prior_strength`, grid `-1…1`): the balanced head is fitted 0.0, which restores `eval/rel_acc_macro_decoder_cal` to 0.466, and an unbalanced one still recovers the classic correction. The same methodology was verified end to end -- on the real graph the train-fitted ensemble weight lands within 0.0001 of the held-out optimum (0.4664 against 0.4665) where the old hardcoded equal weighting lost 0.02 -- and both fitted scalars are stored in the checkpoint config and restored by `RelationFlow.load`, so a loaded model mixes and corrects its evidences exactly as its report measured them. Ablated the F4 connection on the trained model (eval-time, same probe): it contributes +0.004 MRR, +0.021 `path_lift` and improves `pce_ratio_k2` 2.20 -> 2.02, so the connection that was numerically dead before 0.3.1 now pays for itself; `--no-connection` keeps the ablation available without editing the curriculum. `report.json` gains `eval/rel_prior_strength`.

Relation naming is calibrated rather than guessed: the ensemble mixed the head and the transport with a hardcoded equal weight, which was right for an unbalanced head and *wrong* for the class-balanced one (0.42 macro against 0.49 for the head alone) -- `fit_relation_ensemble_weight` now fits that single weight on train edges (grid search, ties to the head), `evaluate()` reports `eval/rel_ensemble_alpha`, and the fitted value is stored in the checkpoint config and restored by `RelationFlow.load`. A run now reports and ships the checkpoint training selected: `<save-dir>/best` is scored with the same `selection_score` as the last epoch (both dial variants, both checkpoints, in `metrics_by_checkpoint`), the better one is re-saved and becomes `final_metrics`, with `shipped_checkpoint` naming it -- previously the report described the weights the last epoch happened to leave behind while the shipped artifact was a different model. `save()` writes the *effective* `use_context` (the curriculum turns the connection on at F4, so a checkpoint labelled `use_context: false` carried a trained `ctx_P` and rebuilt as a different architecture). Fixed: batched `torch.linalg.solve` returns invalid LU pivots on multi-threaded Intel oneMKL (`Pivots given to lu_solve...`, oneMKL `SLASWP`), which made every CPU inference of a 256-d model crash; the operator detects it once and switches to `lstsq` (verified equal to `solve` to 1e-6, rotations orthogonal to 3e-6), falling back to per-matrix solves if that is unavailable too. New CLI: `--contrast-temperature` (the softmax temperature of `L_con`/`L_pull` was hardcoded at 0.07). `report.json` gained a `history` key: every epoch's loss terms and eval metrics, so a term that silently died is visible after the run instead of only in the console scrollback.

New tests: `tests/test_prediction_quality.py` (transport-score exactness on both paths, relation modes, C supervision, C detach, exhaustive diamonds vs brute force, dial calibration, dial LR group, toy trainer).

Third-round pass, measurement protocol. **Every ranking metric and the λ fit counted the query's own row as a candidate answer.** The Słowosieć has **0 self-loops in 359 818 edges** -- `a -r-> b` is never answered by `a`, and a retriever never returns the query document -- but `a` stayed in the index, where it is the candidate certain to be wrong and, of all of them, the one the served query resembles most (`(1-lam_r)*x_a + lam_r*export(...)` still points at `x_a` for small `lam_r`). Consequences: `mrr`-type numbers were understated by the probes where the source outranked the gold (worst on the relations the raw embedding already solves), and the dial was paid to move *away from `x_a`* rather than to find the answer -- the fit over-rewrote exactly the near-synonym relations, which is why `fuzzynimia` lost QR-Recall (0.316 -> 0.299) while `prod_mrr` rose. The tell was that `L_con`/`L_pull` masked the subject and QR-Recall excluded it (`exclude_self=True`); only the metric and the dial fit did not. Fix: `filtered_ranking(..., never_valid=)` plus `WiazkaTrainer._rank_mask` as the single source of truth for `evaluate()`, `calibrate_transport_dial()` and `per_relation_report()`; the gold column is never masked even when a caller lists it. Also: the dial scan now reads 256 edges per relation and takes the argmax of a centred 3-point moving average (`smooth_curve`) -- the old 96-edge single-point argmax could be decided by one probe edge, and a centred average preserves monotone order so `lam = 0` stays reachable -- and `qr_recall(other_map=)` filters other golds against the graph's answer table instead of whatever the sample happened to list (a subject with 40 correct synonyms, sampled once, previously scored its other 39 as misses). New tests: query-row masking and its gold exception, `_rank_mask` protocol, `lam = 0` scored as no-rewriting, `smooth_curve` spike/boundary behaviour, graph-level QR filtering.

Measured on the same weights (one probe, two protocols), the ranking fix moves `raw_mrr` 0.0914 -> 0.1399, `mrr` 0.2010 -> 0.2269, `mrr_macro` 0.1911 -> 0.2113, `prod_mrr` 0.1802 -> 0.2176, `hits@1` 0.0986 -> 0.1357, and lowers the apparent lift (`mrr - raw` 0.110 -> 0.087): part of what had looked like the transport's advantage was the model being paid to escape its own embedding. The refit is the part that was not cosmetic -- on train-edge fits scored on held-out edges, `synonimia` `lam` 0.20 -> 0.10 takes production MRR 0.4387 -> **0.6414** and `fuzzynimia` 0.40 -> 0.30 takes 0.2845 -> **0.3703** (`antonimia właściwa`/`derywacyjność` unchanged at 0.7). `synonimia` is 49.7 % of the edges, yet in the stratified probe all of that is worth +0.004 `prod_mrr`, because the stratified average gives it 1/56 of the vote -- the blind spot that kept the defect invisible. `evaluate()` therefore also reports `eval/prod_mrr_traffic` / `eval/raw_mrr_traffic` / `eval/mrr_prod_lift_traffic` (the same production query on a probe drawn as traffic is distributed): on the shipped weights of a finished run the lift over cosine goes **+0.007 -> +0.040** with no retraining at all, i.e. what was deployed as a query rewriter was almost indistinguishable from doing nothing. Fixed: :func:`load_checkpoint` returned an *uncalibrated* model -- the two calibration scalars are attributes rather than parameters, so they are absent from the state dict and were restored only by `RelationFlow.load`; the constructor default prior of an unweighted head applied to a class-balanced one leaves macro naming accuracy **0.05** instead of 0.47 for every caller of the low-level loader (measured, and now asserted by a test). One shared `apply_calibrated_scalars` is called by the checkpoint loader, so no path can hand back a model that scores differently from its report, and `WiazkaTrainer.load_weights_from` copies the scalars as well. Measured ceiling of the dial recipe: refitting the scan on a 0.05 grid is *worse* than the 0.1 grid (traffic-micro held-out 0.4848 against 0.4870 -- the extra resolution is spent on noise), and the best per-relation `lam` chosen with hindsight on held-out edges is worth 0.4898, i.e. the shipped fit is within 0.003 of it -- the dial is finished as a knob, and further quality has to come from the transport itself.
| **0.3.0** | WIĄZKA training-quality pass. Relation-aware normalised GAT aggregation (replaces the unnormalised, relation-blind sum). Frozen orthonormal PCA base↔operator chart with a tied export (`to_retriever = base_to_op^T`) plus a gated `export_proj` residual — the export is now an exact isometry until `L_pull` earns a deformation (previously QR-Recall measured 0.0 for every relation). Honest evaluation protocol: stratified `(source, relation)` probe scored under `mrr` / `id_mrr` / `raw_mrr` / `prod_mrr` so lifts are always measured within one index; per-relation table in `report.json`.
